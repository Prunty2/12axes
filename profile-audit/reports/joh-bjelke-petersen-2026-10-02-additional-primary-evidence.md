# Joh Bjelke-Petersen — additional primary evidence, 2026-10-02

Historical scope: Queensland premiership **1968–1987**. This review supplements the [full question ledger](joh-bjelke-petersen-2026-10-02-full-audit.md) with additional contemporaneous records. It preserves the earlier [interpretation-ranges report](joh-bjelke-petersen-2026-10-02-calibration-ranges.md) as written. No profile answers, runtime data, state or archetypes were edited. No alternative scores were calculated.

The records distinguish coercive government from abandonment of every civil legal procedure, state primacy from rejection of all municipal lawmaking, and Christian public morality from clerical government. They also provide direct evidence of opposition to same-sex marriage and adoption rights, removing an earlier uncertainty about those precise subjects.

## Additional primary records

**P1 — 9 March 1982, Commonwealth Games Bill, pp4563–65.** Police minister **Russ Hinze**, rather than Joh personally, defended the government bill’s arrest provisions. Clause 36 was agreed with police-station detention, potential bail and subsequent court process. Hinze said the accused had to be brought before a magistrate as soon as practicable. Clause 39 nevertheless authorised extensive compelled identification. This is evidence of government procedure, not a guarantee of good police conduct or a statute on modern preventive terrorism detention. [Queensland Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1982/1982_03_09.pdf).

**P2 — 11 October 1984, pp1380–82.** Local government minister **Russ Hinze** described municipal self-government and broad by-law powers, aiming to minimise state controls. He enumerated enacted powers over planning, services and neighbourhood building requirements. These remained delegated powers under state law. In the same sitting, **p1369**, the justice minister explained that prosecutions of police officers were discontinued where evidence could not sustain charges at trial. This is an official explanation, not independent proof that every discontinuance was justified. [Queensland Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1984/1984_10_11.pdf).

**P3 — 6 March 1985, pp3746–47, Joh’s own statement.** He defended common-law safeguards and explicitly opposed equal marriage and adoption rights for non-heterosexual unions on Christian grounds. His accusations about the proposed bill are treated as partisan preferences, not neutral legal findings. [Queensland Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1985/1985_03_06.pdf).

**P4 — 27 November 1985, pp2843–46, Joh’s own ministerial statement.** He defended elected parliamentary authority, criticised expansive compulsory investigative demands, and opposed courts displacing parliamentary moral and protest standards. This qualifies any inference that defending ordinary courts meant endorsing broad judicial political review. It also demonstrates civil legislative authority rather than government by a church hierarchy. His claims about the proposed commission’s powers are not adopted as neutral legal findings. [Queensland Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1985/1985_11_27.pdf).

**P5 — 23 September 1980, joint Fraser/Joh announcement.** Commonwealth, state and local authorities agreed on drought-water assistance, with Queensland administering assistance for local facilities. This documents practical cooperation among three levels of government, not a claim that Joh accepted unrestricted Commonwealth direction. [Department of the Prime Minister and Cabinet, transcript 5452](https://pmtranscripts.pmc.gov.au/release/transcript-5452).

**A1 — official archival finding, not the underlying primary minute.** Jonathan Richards’ **November 2005** report for Queensland State Archives, p2, identifies Cabinet decision **22913, 5 August 1975**, rejecting water fluoridation. The underlying minute’s reasoning was not examined, so the finding cannot be described as a personal objection to vaccination or all compulsory medicine. [Queensland State Archives report on 1975 Cabinet minutes](https://www.publications.qld.gov.au/dataset/76d9d6d4-9749-4fcc-a19f-482eecb613af/resource/24286b76-2627-49ea-aad0-dbb8dd88aa53/download/ggp-qsa-bnetestwebsiteqld-state-archiveswww.archives.qld.gov.auresearcherscabinetminutesdocument.pdf).

## Evidence-based exclusions and qualifications

- **Same-sex adoption and marriage (`moral_01/06`, current DT/CT):** P3 directly addresses both institutions. The earlier D/C rationales treating them as merely modern projections are unsound; **this review does not approve those relaxations**. The earlier report remains an immutable record.
- **Religious marriage (`religiao_14`):** P3 strengthens the existing permitted CT interpretation, without proving a confessional state.
- **Judicial review (`representacao_05`, current D):** Ordinary common-law protections in P3 do not override P4’s explicit opposition to judicial political intervention. No C or N is approved.
- **Religion versus a contrary law (`religiao_18`, current C):** Neither P3 nor P4 proves how he would resolve every conflict between his own faith and a binding civil law. Parliamentary Christian legislation and opposition to activist churches do not establish blanket private-law obedience or disobedience. **No additional grade is approved.** Public and personal faith commitments remain fixed.
- **Vaccination (`poder_13`, current C):** A1 rebuts a simplistic inference that every public-health intervention was embraced. It does not establish the reason for rejecting fluoridation, the treatment of infectious-disease risks, or a vaccination-consent rule. **No additional grade is approved** from fluoridation alone; no anti-vaccination platform is attributed.
- **Municipal versus federal education (`estrutura_19`, current D):** P2 concerns local administration and by-laws, not municipal administration of schools. P5 concerns drought water. **No additional grade is approved** for education.
- **Federal cooperation:** P5 limits descriptions of permanent opposition to all federal involvement. It does not determine tax-retention fractions, make the prime minister superior in every state dispute, or negate documented state-rights opposition. No additional grade is approved from receipt of a conditional grant alone.
- **Compelled phone unlocking and digital-payment tracking (`poder_20/18`):** P1’s compelled identification cannot be silently equated with a smartphone password or every financial transaction. Conversely P4’s attack on a federal commission is not a general objection to police investigations. No additional grades are approved.
- **Archetypes, trade, hospitals and inheritance:** unchanged. These records do not justify revising the five archetypes, reopening trade answers, denying free public hospitals or weakening protection of inheritance.

The ministerial records are evidence of the government’s stated laws and policy, while Joh’s own statements identify his political preferences. Conflicts between declarations and practical conduct remain visible. No quotation from another politician is silently presented as Joh’s own words, and no later premier’s reforms are credited to him.
