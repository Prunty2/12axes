### Dated primary evidence and competing interpretations

- **T1 — 6 October 1977, Joh's own ministerial statement, p.1248:** he welcomed the International Sugar Agreement's export quotas, price range and reserve stocks. This was managed commodity trade to safeguard producers, rather than a general free-trade agreement. [Queensland Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1977/1977_10_06.pdf).
- **T2 — 21 August 1985, Joh's own statement, pp.15–16:** he reported $31 million in assistance and sought federal price support for sugar, drawing an explicit comparison with national assistance to steel and cars. This supports industrial survival and sectoral protection, without establishing self-sufficiency in every product. [Queensland Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1985/1985_08_21.pdf).
- **T3 — 15 October 1974, Development Minister Fred Campbell, pp.1311–12:** the minister criticised tariff cuts threatening textile firms and their workers, and described his representations for relief. This is evidence of the administration's protective policy, not a quotation from Joh personally. [Queensland Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1974/1974_10_15.pdf).
- **T4 — 26 October 1982, Transport Minister Don Lane, pp.1765–66:** government tender assessment added a 15% preference penalty to overseas bids. A New Zealand supplier still won because it remained substantially cheaper after the preference and federal customs duty. This verifies an actual local purchasing preference, while refuting an absolute domestic-only purchasing rule. [Queensland Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1982/1982_10_26.pdf).
- **T5 — 24 November 1982, Joh himself, p.2682:** he linked export sales to reciprocal admission of some foreign goods, acknowledged cheaper imports, and qualified Buy Queensland Made when Australian goods were three times the price. This is strong counterevidence against broad import closure or always putting sovereignty above consumer prices. [Queensland Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1982/1982_11_24.pdf).
- **T6 — 13 October 1976, Joh himself, p.898:** he refused a proposal to harm foreign mining companies in retaliation for US restrictions on Australian sugar and beef, citing employment. Foreign capital and multinational operation remain strongly supported. [Queensland Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1976/1976_10_13.PDF).
- **T7 — 8 April 1976, Primary Industries Minister, pp.3531–32:** the administration sought protective tariffs for processed potatoes and temporary prohibition of orange-juice imports. This confirms that agricultural protection included import measures, beyond domestic subsidies. It is ministerial policy evidence, not Joh's personal words. [Queensland Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1976/1976_04_08.pdf).
- **T8 — 4 October 1978, Joh himself, p.1993:** he recognised the need to keep industries operating but objected to unequal interstate benefits from tariffs and other protection, seeking recognition in federal financial arrangements. This supports selective, regional bargaining; it does not establish that he endorsed every national tariff. [Queensland Hansard](https://documents.parliament.qld.gov.au/events/han/1978/1978_10_04.pdf).

The original 1968 mineral development and 1987 Romanian export evidence continues to support outward trade. Import tariffs were a Commonwealth responsibility; state procurement preferences and advocacy for federal protection reveal political preferences without assigning the premier powers he did not have. The 1992 anti-tariff-cut speeches by **Senator Flo Bjelke-Petersen** were excluded from Joh's personal record. Neither Flo's positions, later National Party policy nor a generic rural stereotype was substituted for his own administration.
